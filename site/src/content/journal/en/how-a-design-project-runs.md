Most people who are about to design a home carry two feelings at once. One is excitement: finally, a house shaped around the way the family lives. The other is a quieter worry. How many decisions will this take? Will the designer actually listen, or arrive with a style already in mind? What happens if something important is missed and only shows up during construction?

That second feeling is reasonable. A residential project asks for a lot of decisions over several months, and most owners go through it only once or twice in their lives. The best remedy is not reassurance. It is knowing, in plain terms, how the work will unfold, what each step is for, and where your voice shapes the result.

This is how a project runs at Maneda Design Studios.

## Step 1: Understand

Every project starts with listening. Before anything is drawn, the studio wants to know who will live in the house and how: the daily routines, how the family hosts, where people need quiet, what is not working in the current home, and what the new one should feel like when you walk in at the end of the day.

In practice, you send the inquiry form and there is a first conversation about the project, the lot and the budget range. If it is a good fit, you receive a written proposal with the scope, the deliverables and a fixed fee set by phase. The schedule starts when the agreement is signed.

Sometimes you know how you want your home to feel before you know what it should look like. That is exactly where this step begins.

## Step 2: Explore

Next, the studio studies the place and the program together. On the site side, that means the lot, zoning, setbacks, flood zone and the jurisdiction's rules, so constraints are found before they can shape the design by surprise. If added pre-work is needed, such as a survey or soils tests, it is recommended here.

On the design side, it means studying how the spaces should relate: what is public and what is private, how the sun and light move across the lot, which views to open toward, and where privacy matters. You provide what you have, such as a survey, plat or deed, and access for a site visit if one is needed. You receive a constraints summary that frames the design. This step typically takes 1 to 3 weeks.

## Step 3: Develop

This is where the house begins to take form. The studio develops early studies and a concept proposal, usually three options, with sketches, plans, elevations and massing studies. Each option shows a different answer to the same question: how the house sits, how light enters, and how the rooms connect.

You review the options, react to them, and choose a direction. Your feedback matters most here, because this is where the big moves are decided. The right design is not always the first idea, and seeing alternatives side by side is often what makes the right one clear. This step typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

## Step 4: Refine

The chosen direction is then refined: room sizes, openings, proportions, materials and finishes. Where a wish runs into a limit of budget, code or structure, the studio explains the limit plainly and shows alternatives, so you are never asked to approve something you do not understand.

You provide decisions on materials and finishes and timely feedback. You receive a design development set and a clear material and finish direction. This step typically takes 4 to 6 weeks, with revision rounds defined in the agreement.

## Step 5: Document

For clients continuing through full design, the approved design is developed into construction documents and coordinated for permit submittal. This is the set your builder uses to price and build the house, so it carries the detail that keeps decisions from being improvised on site. Where structural drawings are required, they are prepared by a structural engineer hired by the owner under a separate contract, and the studio coordinates with the engineer.

Construction documents typically take 8 to 12 weeks, then permitting takes 3 to 8 weeks, depending on the jurisdiction. You receive the construction document set and support through permit review comments.

## How long it takes, and how payment works

For a single-family residence of about 2,000 to 3,500 SF, the path from the first research through construction documents usually takes 16 to 24 weeks, assuming your approvals come within about 5 business days at each step.

Each phase has its own fixed fee, agreed in writing before work starts. Each phase fee is invoiced in two parts: half to start the phase and half on delivery. Invoices are due within 7 days, and the next phase begins when the invoice is paid. If a project pauses after a phase, you have paid for that phase only.

## Why the process starts with people

A clear process saves time on both sides, but that is not the main reason it exists. Its purpose is to make sure the house reflects the people who will live in it, not a style imposed on them, and not a plan drawn for the market.

Many homes are designed quickly, from a template or a trend, and look good in photographs. The trouble shows up later, in a kitchen that does not fit how the family cooks, a living room no one uses, or a decision left for the construction site that should have been made on paper. Every step above exists to prevent that: to understand before drawing, to test options before committing, to explain limits before they become surprises, and to document the design carefully enough that it can be built as intended.
