Buying a lot is the cheapest moment to learn what it cannot hold. A site analysis answers that question in writing before you commit, and before anyone draws a house.

## What the rules can limit

Four things usually decide how much house a lot can hold:

- **Zoning.** The district sets what you can build and how much of the lot you can cover.
- **Setbacks.** The required distances from each property line shape the buildable area.
- **Flood zone.** It can affect where the house sits and how it is built.
- **Access.** The driveway, the road, and the utilities decide what is practical.

## What a site analysis covers

A site analysis is built from public records and a site visit. It covers pre-visit research on the zoning district, setbacks, lot coverage, height limits, and open space requirements. It adds an aerial and GIS overlay review, a basic flood zone check, and notes on access, drainage, views, sun path, noise, privacy, topography, and tree cover. You receive a written report with the opportunities and constraints, a zoning summary table, and annotated photos.

## What it does not cover

It is an informal zoning summary for design planning. It is not a survey, a legal opinion, or an engineering study, and it does not replace soil testing. Final development rights must be confirmed by a licensed professional or the local building department.

## When to order one

Order it before you buy the lot, or before you design on a lot you already own. The fixed prices are on the services page: one for lots under half an acre and one for larger lots.
