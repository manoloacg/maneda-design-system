You want to add a bedroom, a primary suite, a bigger kitchen or a second floor. Before anyone draws anything, one question decides who can do the work: does Florida require an architect for your addition? For most one-family and two-family homes, the answer is often no, but "often" is not a plan. This article explains the rule in plain terms, what it leaves out, and how to get a firm answer for your own project. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The short answer

Florida law protects the title "architect" and controls who may practice architecture. It also carves out an exemption for certain residential buildings. Under that exemption, plans for qualifying one-family and two-family residences do not have to be prepared by an architect. A residential designer, a builder, or the owner may prepare them, as long as everything else the law and the building code require is met. The exemption does not decide everything. Engineering rules, the building code and your local building department still apply.

## What the exemption does not cover

**Engineering.** The exemption is from the architecture law only. Engineering is governed by Florida Statutes chapter 471. Many additions need structural drawings sealed by a licensed engineer: the foundation, the framing, the connection to the existing house, and the design for wind loads. In Florida, wind design is a large part of residential structural work. [VERIFY: whether you want to name the Florida Building Code chapter on wind loads.]

**The building code.** The Florida Building Code applies to the addition whoever draws it. The drawings must show how the work meets it.

**Other building types.** The exemption is written for houses. Commercial buildings, mixed-use buildings and multifamily buildings beyond the exemption are a different matter. [VERIFY: where the line falls for multifamily.]

**Local requirements.** Your building department reviews the permit application and decides what it needs to see, within the limits set by state law. Some ask for more than others.

## Who seals what on a typical addition

On many residential additions in Florida, the drawings come from more than one person:

- **The designer** prepares the plans, elevations, sections and details that show the addition.
- **A structural engineer** prepares and seals the structural drawings. The owner usually hires the engineer directly, under a separate contract.
- **Other engineers or contractors** may design mechanical, electrical or plumbing systems, depending on the scope and the jurisdiction.

Where a seal is required, drawings are reviewed and sealed by a licensed professional of record. The person who seals takes professional responsibility for the sealed work. A seal is not a stamp of approval on someone else's whole set. Always ask what a seal covers.

## When an addition may need more than the exemption

Check early if any of these apply. Each one can change who must prepare or seal the drawings, or what the building department asks for. Mark each as a question for your building department, not a conclusion.

- The house is a condominium unit, or the addition touches a shared wall or roof.
- The addition creates a separate dwelling unit.
- The property is in a historic district or under a local overlay.
- The property is in a flood zone, where floor elevations and construction methods have their own rules.
- The addition is large compared with the existing house. [VERIFY: whether any Florida rule ties professional requirements to size or cost for residential work.]

## Questions to ask your building department

Call or email the building department that covers your address. In the Orlando area, that may be the city, not the county. Ask:

1. Does my addition need drawings sealed by an architect, an engineer, or both?
2. Which sheets need a seal?
3. What does a complete permit application for a residential addition include?
4. Do you need a survey, and how recent must it be?
5. Are there local amendments or overlays that apply to my lot?

Ask for the answers in writing if you can, and keep them with your project file.
