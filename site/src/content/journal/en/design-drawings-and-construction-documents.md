A residential project moves through two kinds of drawings. They look similar and do different jobs.

## Design drawings

Design work runs from predesign, through schematic design, to design development. Predesign takes 1 to 3 weeks, schematic design 4 to 6 weeks, and design development 4 to 6 weeks. You end with plans, elevations, sections, 3D views, and material and finish selections. This is a resolved design. It is not permit-ready and it is not for construction.

## Construction documents

Once you approve the design, it is developed into construction documents, so the design you approved is the design that gets built. This step takes 8 to 12 weeks. It is the set that goes to the jurisdiction and the builder.

## Who seals the drawings

Where a seal is required, drawings are reviewed and sealed by a licensed professional of record. On residential projects this is often the structural engineer, hired and paid by the owner under a separate contract. The studio coordinates the drawings with the engineer during construction documents.

## Permitting

After the set is submitted, permitting typically takes 3 to 8 weeks. The jurisdiction sets the review time, not the studio.

## Plan the total time

For a single-family residence of about 2,000 to 3,500 SF, predesign through construction documents takes 16 to 24 weeks, assuming your approvals come within 5 business days.
