## Architect or residential designer: which does your home need?

Many homeowners search for an architect when what they need is a home designed, drawings made, and a permit approved. The answer depends on the state, the building, and who must put a seal on the drawings.

### What an architect is
An architect is a person licensed by a state board. The title is protected by law. Only a licensed person may use it, and the rules differ by state.

### What the law often allows for houses
Some states do not require an architect for plans of a one- or two-family home. Florida is one example, under Florida Statutes 481.229. Ohio has a similar residential exemption under Ohio Revised Code 4703.18. Other states do not. Check the rule for your state before you hire anyone.

### What a residential designer does
A residential designer develops the design: the site analysis, the plan, the elevations, the 3D views, the materials, and the construction documents. A designer is not licensed as an architect and does not use that title.

### Who seals the drawings
Where a seal is required, drawings are reviewed and sealed by a licensed professional of record. On many residential projects this is the structural engineer, hired and paid by the owner under a separate contract. Whether an architect's seal is also needed depends on the jurisdiction and the building.

### How to decide
- Ask your building department what it requires for your project.
- Ask what the professional of record will review and seal.
- Ask who coordinates the engineer and the drawings.
- Choose the person whose work you have seen and whose process you understand.
